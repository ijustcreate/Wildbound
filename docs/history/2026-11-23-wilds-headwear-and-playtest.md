# Headwear and Wild Lands playtest changes

The 30 remaining headwear designs have new 24 × 24 inventory icons and new fitted art in eight directions. The four witch hats now sit lower and use a slimmer rim and a slightly smaller silhouette. The existing skull pivot, authored pixel overrides, profiles, and inventory behavior remain in use. The [headwear study index](../art/concepts/headwear-refresh.md) links all 34 concept designs. The local comparison video uses the previous build and the updated native renderer at matching scale, pose, and direction.

- Holding aim uses a single colored guide without the dark rails. Ranged guides start at the visible release point and trace the existing projectile blockers. The sword guide follows the dash footprint.
- The zombie face has grouped sockets, a readable brow and a separate jaw treatment. The wolf tail tapers through its original joints. The black spider's imported walk advances over its actual short steps. Boars carry more motion through the rear during their rush. A landed rush pushes the hero 18 world pixels along its committed direction through normal movement collision.
- Wet landings and wading add expanding distortion to the existing low-resolution reflection pass. Near-surface swimmers distort the reflected texture with their wake. The shared pass uses one water texture sample and a bounded set of active impulses. Bare ice reflections are faint and still, with snow and snow shelves clipped out.
- Water shelves have varied pixel-stepped depth edges and fragmented moving crests. Railway supports paint below the deck, with ripples where their feet meet water. The Woodland footbridge shares the swimmer reveal. A jump approaching the deck preserves its height as the player clears the edge. The underwater air bar is visible in the railway renderer, and bubble marks are sparse and fade with depth and age.
- Bayou plants are smaller and denser. Fresh ambient water-elemental spawns are less common, while stored encounters and deliberate encounters retain their original behavior. The existing mossy shrines open persistent Caverns rooms by Interact, with an exact return to each player's approach position.
- Ice has colder chest artwork, exposed and snow-bound shoreline detail, varied logs and crystal clusters, snow footprints for players and enemies, and local melting from fire impacts. The central Homestead stays on snow-free ground at its existing location.
- Wild Lands contacts render below actors on their actual support surface, including the bridge and train deck. Trees use crisp root sampling and bounded falling leaf effects. Foreground tufts overlap rocks and mushrooms. Walking past mushrooms bends the nearby tufts briefly. Bushes have different silhouettes, greens and small flowers or berries. Direct fire singes a bush locally and leaves a small scorch and leaf debris.
- Clocks are smaller and use walls or visible posts. The rug, settled debris, and ground shadow draw beneath vehicles. The floating exit label was removed and the exit control still works.
- The Overlook hedge maze has richer clipped foliage and a south entrance alongside the existing north entrance. Damage indices remain stable outside the small opening. The center chest takes priority over nearby loose loot and reports a full bag or a previously claimed axe. Vehicle impacts use the existing bumper and speed rules to damage contacted hedge sections.

## Crowd measurements

The selected speedup avoids repeated geometry and train pose work during collision. The frozen muted Electron fixture compared the same mixed 5-, 10-, and 20-enemy encounters. Median update cost fell from 19.3 to 9.5 ms, 28.5 to 13.0 ms, and 79.5 to 15.2 ms, respectively. Actor trajectories, health, attacks, and sampled collision probe counts matched. This measures CPU update cost. Rendering remains a separate cost at high actor counts, and gameplay FPS was unmeasured.

The intermittent selection issue reported in the lobby could not be reproduced. In the packaged lobby, a test explorer selected through the normal character picker spawned, equipped the saved hat, and rendered visibly. The isolated test showed zero renderer errors.
